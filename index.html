Ring of Fire: Plot the Volcanoes

Real grid coordinates, real volcanoes. Find each one on the map by its column and row — no printing, no partner needed. Class 4A, Mr. Jay.

How to read a grid

1

Find the column. A location like "6, Y" gives the column NUMBER first — count across the top.

2

Find the row. The LETTER comes second — count down the side.

3

Drop the pin. Where the column and row meet is the exact spot — that's where Kilauea volcano is on the real map!

Move your mouse (or finger) over any map below — a little label will always tell you which column and row you're pointing at.

Source: Mystery Science, "Could a volcano pop up where you live?"

    Look back through all four continents — the dots trace a giant horseshoe around the edge of the Pacific Ocean. About 75% of the world's active volcanoes sit along this belt. It's no coincidence: it follows the edges of the tectonic plates underneath the Pacific.

    Talk about it: which continent had the most volcanoes close together? Why do you think they cluster in lines instead of being spread out evenly?
